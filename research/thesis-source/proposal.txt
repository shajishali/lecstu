1.Problem Statement 
The current apps identified to support student-faculty interaction at universities, while they exist, 
are all limited and regimented in form and infrequently used by students as they search for 
assistance or information, most still use help desks or a webs ite. Artificial intelligence (AI) 
implementations such as chat boots, voice-active agents, multilingual natural language processing 
(NLP), and predictive scheduling, previously when AI could not be a part of the solutions because 
students would defer acces s to faculty information or scheduling, will be more of a universal 
challenge for students to face with language barriers or disabilities. Today we can explore this 
issue as a research question with enhanced AI like advanced speech modeling (e.g. Whisper), 
transformer-based question answering; and more on predictive scheduling with the idea of 
transforming this university specific problem into a research question i.e. where are the AI agents 
have been tested in a research context; and devising a strategy of  a manner in which AI could be 
systematically implemented at scale and inclusively with the higher education space. 
2.Literature Review and Identified Gaps 
Previous research and programs have demonstrated aspects of our vision, but there are significant 
gaps that this project intends to fill: 
• Restricted Usefulness of Campus Apps:  Many universities’ mobile apps have limited 
capabilities and engagement with students [1]. For example, a 2021 study showed that “the 
majority of current apps used across Universities ... are limited in their capabilities and not 
very popular among students” [ 1]. Gap: Several apps remain unintegrated (faculty 
directory, campus maps, notifications). Solution: Our solution integrates important 
services (faculty directory, announcements, campus map) into one app to be useful and 
used. 
• Voice Interaction Capability Exists and Is Underutilized:  Students often use GUIs or 
text-only chat interfaces on school portals, and voice interfaces have rarely been applied.  
However, research on voice assistants indicates positive aspects: voice assistants allow 
users to multitask, provide a more natural interaction, typically have higher accuracy 
features than text-based interfaces [4] [5]. For example, in their research, El lucian states: 
"Voice interaction is something that is especially interesting a nd engaging, ... a 
conversation will always, by its nature, provide a richer experience ... a relatively greater 
depth of emotional satisfaction" [4]. Gap: Voice command and speech recognition 
functions have not been built into university systems. Solution: Integrate ASR (speech-to-
text) and TTS (text -to-speech) so that it is hands -free. We will use a leading ASR (e.g., 
OpenAI's Whisper, which was trained on 680,000+ hours of multilingual data [6]) to 
accomplish this. 
• Monolingual Interfaces: Many campuses are linguistically diverse, but student support 
apps are often only in one language. Ralston et al. developed a multilingual voice chatbot 
by integrating IBM Watson into a student support system [7]. Gap: Existing solutions 
normally do not offer multilingual or multi-dialect services. Solution: Our chatbot design

includes multilingual support with NLP. An example of this would be Rasa's customizable 
pipelines with support for several languages [8]. The system will offer English and one or

• Scaling Peer Mentor Usage: A literature review found universities are slow to transition 
to virtual peer-to-peer interactions despite benefits (24/7 access, familiarity) [6] [2]. In fact,

the app where an intelligent virtual peer will respond to common campus-related questions. 
The mentor space will allow for both text and video so the virtual peer can assist students 
anywhere. 
• Siloed “Smart” Systems: Some projects (e.g., a smart mirror information kiosk [9]) show 
the potential of voice assistant technology on campus, but they remain location -based 
devices. Gap: one cannot use the information kiosk off-campus. Solution: Ensure students 
have the same smart -assistant capabilities anywhere (on - or off -campus) via mobile or 
web-based systems. 
Each of these gaps —feature-limited apps  [1], no voice/multilingual functionality  [4] [8] , and 
underutilized chatbots [2]—will be addressed by the proposed system, integrating them into a 
single platform leveraging modern AI. 
3.Aims and Objectives 
This project aims not only to design and develop a smart, scalable, web - and mobile-responsive 
app for faculty access and student support, but also to study and evaluate how AI capabilities can 
enhance inclusiveness, efficiency, and personalization in high er education. It will systematically 
integrate AI -enhanced features —multilingual voice interaction, AI -based timetable parsing, 
retrieval-augmented Q&A, and predictive scheduling —into one consolidated platform. 
Specifically, the project will benchmark a variety of AI models (e.g., Whisper vs. Google Speech 
for voice recognition; GPT -4 vs. BERT for Q&A; reinforcement learning vs. optimization for 
scheduling); assess their performance on campus; and iteratively identify the best combinations of 
tools to impro ve faculty -student communication, campus navigation, and management of 
academic/non-academic tasks. 
Objectives: 
1. User Interface and Accessibility: Developing a responsive user interface (UI) for 
students, lecturers, and administrators on web and mobile . Implement role-based access 
(RBAC) for different user types (students, staff, admins) . Making accessibility compliant 
(WCAG compliant, multilingual etc.) 
2. Intelligent Timetable and Course Content : Allow Faculty to upload class/appointment 
timetables. Allow students to auto -generate individual timetables. Communicate anytime 
there is a change to a class, or class is cancelled, and provide live notifications.

4.Methodology 
The project will follow a user -centered iterative development approach, where research 
experimentation and evaluation is key. Phase 1 (Months 1-2): Requirements analysis, stakeholder 
interviews, and an initial system design for interface and functionality. Create wireframes and user 
interface prototypes for a mobile/web interface, then implement the wireframed interfaces with 
functionality for each interface in Agile Sprints. Each phase of the project includes research tasks 
(benchmark metrics of ASR models  on accuracy, comparing methods of timetable and calendar 
parsing, assessing the NLP quality of chatbot applications, etc.).  
The key implementation phases include back -end application programming interface, front -end 
user interface, chatbot and voice interface applications, and predictive scheduling. Each of the 
above listed features will include user studies and experiments wit h a sampling of faculty and 
student users to collect usability feedback and assess model outputs. Outcomes from studies and 
experiments will be documented, including research notes, experiment results, results of 
performance comparisons, and usability of findings. 
Standard software engineering aspects (version control, peer -review, automated testing) will 
provide quality to the technological system, while the research aspects and methodology will 
provide scientific rigor around the measurement and evaluation, and contribute to replicability. By 
the End of Month 12 the system will be fully test, ready for cloud deployment, and piloted to 
collect empirical data for publication, as well as practical validation.

2.AI and NLP Tools 
This project entails the integration of several different AI components that we have assessed with 
global alternatives to find the best suited match for application in the higher education context: 
• Chatbot Framework (Rasa): Chatbot Framework (Rasa): We selected Rasa because it is 
open-source, readily customizable, and supports multilingual NLU pipelines. Furthermore, 
we have found through prior work efforts (see refs), that most university chatbots are rule-
based and narrow. Rasa enables custom tokenizers, API integration, and retraining using 
university-specific data (i.e., FAQs, schedules, policies) which is not possible with closed-
source platforms we evaluated. We will test Rasa pipelines against other framework 
options (Dialogflow, IBM Watson Assistant) along the lines of multilingual options, 
accuracy, and most importantly, usefulness to students. 
• Language Models (GPT -4 and Transformers):  Language Models (GPT -4 and 
Transformers): "Lead Little Models" (LLMs) such as GPT -4 track better on more 
complicated Question and Answer (Q&A), but generate narrative (ex. course details, policy 
orientations) richer in context [2]. However, there are relat ed concerns about bias, 
accuracy, and privacy [3]. We will benchmark comparing GPT -4 to open -source 
transformer models (Bert, Robert, etc.). and benchmark retrieval -augmented Q&A. The 
research question we are posing is whether GPT-4 would significantly outpace lightweight, 
on-device models for academic Q&A and associated trade -offs with quality, latency and 
privacy. 
• Speech-to-Text (Whisper vs Cloud APIs): Whisper (OpenAI) is a multilingual ASR 
trained on 680,000 hours of audio and robust to accent and noise [6]. Whisper can run 
locally (no vendor lock-in) unlike proprietary APIs (Google, Azure, AWS). We will also 
compare Whisper to commercial ASR APIs on ac curacy, latency, and multilingual 
performance in noisy campus scenarios. This study is warranted because there is limited 
literature systematically addressing ASR in higher education.

6.Ethics and Privacy 
Since the system will be able to keep personal data and use voice data, ethics and privacy breach 
will need to be considered. To establish trust with the user and uphold principles for responsible 
AI, we will implement a range of policies and safeguards: 
Data Management Procedures: 
• Where data will be stored: All user data (login information, student schedules, and usage 
logs) will be stored in a secure, encrypted database provided by a reliable cloud service 
(e.g., Firebase/Atlas) and in compliance with relevant data protection regulations. 
• Data retention period: The exact data will be retained only for as long as necessary (e.g., 
a single academic year, or project cycle) and deleted/anonymized immediately when the 
necessary period has concluded. 
• Access control: Data can only be accessed by authorized users based on their position 
(e.g., student, lecturer, administrator) with access to relevant parts of the data. Developers 
and system administrators will have limited access for debugging or maintenance purposes 
based on role-based authentication. 
• How voice input is handled: Voice commands will always be at player's control (e.g., will 
be indicator lights for "recording," will always be an explicit step to opt in), and raw audio 
saved to baseline will be held perpetuity unless it is needed to improve the system (with 
informed consent). Users can mute microphones and/or disable microphones, so that 
"earshot" recording is not accidentally recorded. 
 Bias and Fairness Measurements: 
We will measure the chatbot to ensure it does not produce biased or harmful content.  
Measurement Metrics: We will measure performance and fairness using language -specific 
metrics, including:                                                                                              
           * The accuracy and error rate of ASR/NLP for Sinhala, Tamil, and English input. 
           * Relevance and appropriateness of responses as rated by raters from different 
               demographic and linguistic identities.
